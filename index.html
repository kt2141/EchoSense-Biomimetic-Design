Discover

A street without sight is a street full of noise

One sense is deprived and another is overloaded. Hearing must track traffic, crowds and prompts, while the cane reaches only about one cane-length ahead.

2.2 billion

people live with a near or distance vision impairment (WHO, World Report on Vision, 2019).

Problem statement

Visually impaired urban pedestrians need to detect obstacles and judge distance early in crowded, noisy streets, but are held back by the loss of sight and an overloaded sense of hearing.

The Sensory Gap

Nothing tells the user how far away an obstacle is beyond cane reach without also occupying hearing. The skin, a silent and always-available channel, is left unused.

What the audit found

  • Hearing carries traffic, voices and navigation prompts at once.
  • The cane covers the ground, not head height or distance.
  • Touch on the wrist is free and private.
Develop

From echolocation to haptics

Bats time the echoes of ultrasonic calls. EchoSense does the same with a 40 kHz sensor, and arranges its motors in the hexagonal cells of an Apis mellifera honeycomb.
